Why it works

⏱️

Anki's scheduler, not an imitation

The app runs a port of Anki's own scheduler with FSRS, tested answer by answer against Anki itself. A word comes back just before you would forget it, so a few minutes a day is enough.

🔊

Hear everything

Every word, its forms, its definition and its example are voiced: 10,386 recordings. The text fills in as the voice reads it, and sentences have their own speed dial.

🇱🇹

Think in Lithuanian

Each word has a second card that gives a definition written entirely in Lithuanian and asks for the word. You learn to recall it, not just to recognise it.

🎯

Built for the exam

Every word is levelled from the official descriptions. Pick your goal, A1, A2 or B1, and new words come level by level, the everyday ones first.

A game board, not a spreadsheet

Gintaro kelias turns the deck into a journey with Gandras the stork: three regions, one for each level, and a stop for every theme. The goal is to study every day and finish the cards that are due: your reviews and a few new words.

  • Choose your pace: 3, 5, 10 or 15 new words a day, with the minutes it takes shown before you start. Change it any time.
  • The Treasury: one piece of amber per word, polished as you remember it, and how much you would recall right now.
  • The Album: a sticker for each theme, bronze to amber as you learn its words.
  • The leaderboard: this week and all time, under a nickname, for players signed in with Google.
  • Your progress on every device: sign in with Google to keep a copy and carry on elsewhere. Without it, progress stays in your browser.
  • Coming from Anki? Import your Anki review history of this deck and pick up where you left off.
  • Light and dark, sound effects and quiet music, all of which can be turned off.
  • Spot a mistake? Tap the flag on the card and say what is wrong; it goes straight to the author.

Two cards per word

Every word makes a recognition card and a production card. The second one is the point of the deck: a definition written entirely in Lithuanian asks you to produce the word, which is why no definition ever contains the word it defines.

What's on a card

  • Audio on everything: the headword, its inflected forms, the Lithuanian definition and the example sentence. Four recordings per word.
  • Stress marks (kirtis) on headwords and on the inflected forms shown, not just the dictionary form.
  • Inflected forms on every card: nouns with the plural, verbs with 3rd person present and past.
  • Forms in coloured bubbles: each form sits in a bubble coloured by what it is, vyr. g. blue, mot. g. pink, vns. green, dgs. amber, with a legend on every back, so the feminine is never read as a plural. Nouns with a form for each gender show the pair (mókytojas mókytoja).
  • Full case tables for the personal pronouns, voiced, including the feminine column that most decks leave out.
  • The target word is marked in the example and in its translation, so it is obvious which word the sentence is teaching.

Grammar words and set phrases

The small words that hold a sentence together get usage cards: how the word is used, the words it is confused with, and a gap to fill in each example. On the map they are a stop of their own, Bendravimas ir gramatika.

  • Greetings: labas, ačiū, prašau, atsiprašau, viso gero
  • Question words: kaip, kas, kur, kada, kodėl, kiek
  • Conjunctions: ir, o, bet, kad, kai, jei, nes
  • Prepositions: į, su, iš, nuo, apie, ant, po
  • Particles: ar, tik, dar, jau, juk, štai
  • kaž- and nie- words: kažkas, niekas, niekada, niekur
  • Pronouns: tas, šis, toks, savo, mano, mūsų
  • It is…: galima, gaila, įdomu, būtina, draudžiama

Questions

Is it really free?

Yes. The app and the deck are free to use, with no ads and no account needed.

Do I need to sign up?

No. Open kelias.valdas.uk and start; your progress is kept in your browser. Sign in with Google only if you want a copy of your progress to carry to another device.

I already use Anki. Can I keep my progress?

If you studied this deck in Anki, export it from Anki and import it in the app's Settings: your review history comes with it and every card keeps its schedule. The deck itself is no longer offered as an Anki download.

Which level should I aim for?

The one your exam or course asks for. Set the goal to A1 or A2 and the app leaves the words above it out until you raise it; B1 is the whole deck, and the default.

Who checks the Lithuanian?

Forms and stress marks are cross-checked against dictionaries; the definitions and examples are written with AI assistance and checked by rules, not yet by a native speaker. See how it was made, and report anything that looks wrong.

How it was made

Honestly, and with the seams showing:

  • The word list follows Pusiaukelė, the official A2 content description (Ramonienė et al., 2006, revised 2016), which the State Commission of the Lithuanian Language publishes free so that teaching materials can be built on it. Its twelve topics are the first twelve themes, and about half of the headwords appear in its word index. The selection, definitions and examples are this deck's own.
  • Inflected forms and stress marks come from English Wiktionary, the VDU Kirčiuoklis stress tool and the phonology_engine library, cross-checked against each other. Every form is verified against the hunspell lt_LT dictionary.
  • Definitions, examples and translations were written with AI assistance and checked by automated rules — no headword inside its own definition, an inflected form in every example, restricted vocabulary, spell-checked throughout. They have not been proofread by a native speaker.
  • The audio is synthesised, not recorded: the Lithuanian synthesiser operated by UAB Intelektika, voice “astra”, built on the publicly funded LIEPA project.

Intelektika's terms of use state that “Mūsų suteiktų Paslaugų rezultatus galite naudoti tiek asmeniniais, tiek komerciniais tikslais” — the results of the service may be used for personal and commercial purposes. No attribution is required; the credit below is courtesy.

Report a mistake

Corrections are very welcome, above all from native speakers. If a definition, example, translation, form, stress mark or recording is wrong, the fix goes into the next version.

  • While you study in Gintaro kelias: tap the flag on the card, pick what is wrong (translation, spelling, audio or something else) and say what it should be. The report knows which card it is about, goes straight to the author, and needs no account.
  • On GitHub, if you would rather report in public: open a correction with the word and what is wrong. It needs a free GitHub account, and you can see what is already reported.
